P2 Sodium oxides 鈉氧化物 (Q4)

On the paper: 100 g Na; mass rises ≈ 35% in air; ≈ 70% in pure O₂.

Key ideas 關鍵概念

  • Mass increase = mass of oxygen added (metal mass unchanged). 35% of 100 g = 35 g O.
  • Find \(n(\mathrm{Na})\) and \(n(\mathrm{O})\), simplify to the lowest whole-number ratio → empirical formula（經驗式）.
  • If \(M_r\) is given, scale the empirical formula to match → molecular formula（分子式）.

Worked example (air · 35%)

n(Na) = 100/23 ≈ 4.35 mol
n(O) = 35/16 ≈ 2.19 mol
Na : O ≈ 4.35 : 2.19 ≈ 2 : 1 → Na₂O

Worked example (pure O₂ · 70%)

n(O) = 70/16 ≈ 4.38 mol
Na : O ≈ 4.35 : 4.38 ≈ 1 : 1 → empirical NaO
If Mr = 78: NaO has Mr=39 → ×2 → molecular Na₂O₂ (sodium peroxide)

Context: air often gives Na₂O; pure oxygen favours peroxide Na₂O₂.

P3 Lead oxide X 鉛氧化物 (Q6)

“Contains 90.6% Pb by mass” → oxygen is the rest (100 − 90.6 = 9.4%).

  1. Assume 100 g sample: Pb = 90.6 g, O = 9.4 g
  2. \(n(\mathrm{Pb})=90.6/207.2 \approx 0.437\); \(n(\mathrm{O})=9.4/16 = 0.5875\)
  3. Divide by the smaller: Pb : O ≈ 1 : 1.343 ≈ 3 : 4
  4. Empirical formula = Pb₃O₄ (red lead)
Check %Pb = (3×207.2) / (3×207.2 + 4×16) × 100% ≈ 90.7% ✓

Bonus: mole ratio PbO to PbO₂

Pb₃O₄ can be written as 2PbO · PbO₂ (two Pb(II) + one Pb(IV)).

PbO : PbO₂ = 2 : 1

P4 Limiting reagent & yield 限量試劑與產率 (Q5)

C₂H₂(g) + 2Br₂(l) → C₂H₂Br₄(l)

Your script: balancing ✅; moles attempted; theoretical yield ≈ 5.61 g (close); actual yield unclear.

Standard steps 標準步驟

  1. Moles: n(C₂H₂)=2.00/26≈0.0769; n(Br₂)=5.20/159.8≈0.0325
  2. Compare with coefficients: 1 mol C₂H₂ needs 2 mol Br₂.
    Using all C₂H₂ would need Br₂ = 0.0769×2≈0.154 > 0.0325 → Br₂ is limiting; C₂H₂ is excess.
  3. Theoretical yield from limiting: n(product)=0.0325/2=0.01625; Mr=345.6 → m≈5.62 g
  4. Actual yield = theoretical × (% yield / 100) = 5.62 × 0.90 ≈ 5.06 g

Write one English sentence in the exam, e.g. Bromine is the limiting reagent / Ethyne is in excess.
